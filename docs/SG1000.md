# SG-1000

Profile `sg1000`: the Sega SG-1000 and SG-1000 II, **experimental, developer only, in development**. This is stages 1 to 3 of the machine
([NEW_MACHINE.md](NEW_MACHINE.md), the converter's side): the converter recognises an SG-1000 cartridge file, reads it, translates it, checks the
translation against the reference interpreter and **delivers a game executable** (the player and the host kit around the translated cartridge). It is
for developers: no player is offered it, no game has been played by a person, and nothing on this page says that a game plays well, that the hardware is
reproduced faithfully or how long input takes to reach the screen. Those are five separate measures, and for this machine only the first, and the
agreement with the reference interpreter, are measured, per conversion, and only on the routes played; the delivered file is replayed headless, not played.

## 1. What is converted, and what is not

The scope below is the one the machine is built for.

| In scope | Out of scope (with the reason) |
| --- | --- |
| SG-1000 and SG-1000 II cartridges (the two consoles run the same software), plain ROM of 2 KiB to 48 KiB, read from a `.sg`, from a `.bin` or `.rom` that you choose as SG-1000 or keep in a folder named `sg1000`, `sg-1000` or `sg_1000`, or from a ZIP holding one | **SC-3000**: its keyboard, its BASIC cartridges and its cassette are another machine's work |
| Two pads (the D-pad and buttons 1 and 2) and the Pause button, which is the console's NMI | **Othello Multivision, Terebi Oekaki** (a graphics tablet), **Dahjee** RAM-expansion cartridges, Card Catcher, Music Editor and the other peripherals and boards: a cartridge that needs one is refused with a clear message |
| NTSC and PAL timing, chosen per game as on the Master System | **SK-1100 keyboard, SP-400 plotter, FM Sound Unit** and paddle-style controllers, unless a first census shows a game that needs one |
| The cartridge RAM of one board of the library studied (The Castle), in a later stage | A file larger than 48 KiB, or not a whole number of kilobytes: a bank-switched or unknown board, refused by the reader |

The reader (`retrorecomp/sg1000_rom.py`) accepts a whole number of kilobytes from 2 KiB to 48 KiB, alone or as the only cartridge of a ZIP. An SG-1000
dump has no header, so a 512-byte copier header is **not** removed: such a file is refused as a size that is not a whole number of kilobytes. The
file is read in place and never changed; Retro-Recomp publishes no game. A cartridge's bytes do not say that it is an SG-1000 cartridge, so the
extension `.sg`, the folder or your choice in the menu decides.

## 2. How it is planned to work

1. **Native code.** The cartridge is translated ahead of time to C by the shared banked Z80 chain (`retrorecomp/z80_chain.py`, the one the Master
   System, the Game Gear and the MSX family use) on the machine's own description, `SG1000_MACHINE` in `retrorecomp/systems/sg1000.py`: eight 8 KiB
   regions of the 48 KiB cartridge space, no wait state on the M1 cycle, /INT read at the end of each instruction and the /NMI pin of the Pause button
   (the chain learned that pin for this machine and the ColecoVision). What the translation does not cover will run on the reference Z80
   interpreter and be counted, per CPU.
2. **The machine.** The TMS9918A video chip is the shared module `native/tms9918/` that the MSX family compiles too; the sound chip, the 1 KiB of RAM,
   the ports and the pads are the machine's own C, to be written. No emulator core is meant to be inside the executable.
3. **No firmware.** An SG-1000 cartridge boots by itself: no BIOS is read, needed or shipped.

What exists today is the profile, the cartridge reader, the machine's description, the machine's C (`native/sg1000_machine`, with the shared
`native/tms9918` and `native/sn76489`), the conversion loop, the game itself (its player `sg1000_player.c`, its host-kit adapter
`native/hostkit/adapters/rrhk_sg1000.inc` and its quick states `sg1000_state_file.inc`, checked ROM-free by `tools/sg1000_player_selftest.py` on an
authored cartridge), the packaging (`prepare_game` in `retrorecomp/sg1000_conversion.py`, the game's CMake target in `retrorecomp/sg1000_build.py`),
the registries and this page. The conversion delivers the game executable (stage 3, below).

### How it converts (stages 2 and 3, developer only)

`retrorecomp/sg1000_conversion.py` runs, in a work folder `.build/sg1000/<sha12>` and with no BIOS and no mapper (the 8 to 48 KiB image is padded
to whole 8 KiB regions with FFh and mirrored by the machine):

1. **The start of the `play` route** is chosen on the reference interpreter (`retrorecomp/sg1000_routes.py`, `route_start.py`): button 1, button 2
   or both pressed 1, 2 or 3 times, the variant whose walk reaches the most pictures the same frames without input never show. The `attract` route
   has no input; the `extended` and `thorough` scan levels add one or two long routes of 18,000 frames, a walk seeded by the cartridge. No route
   presses Pause.
2. **Translation and learning** (`retrorecomp/sg1000_build.py`, on `retrorecomp/z80_chain_build.py`): the generator translates the cartridge, the
   native check is built (`sg1000_check`) beside the reference runner (`sg1000_reference`), every route is run on the check, and the code the
   interpreter ran from RAM is learned and translated with byte guards on the next pass (at most three passes).
3. **The comparison.** The native check and the reference run every route; every compared field (CPU registers, cycles, /INT and /NMI counts, RAM, VRAM, the
   picture of every frame, the VDP registers, the sound and the PSG, the watched bytes) and every frame's row must be equal, or the conversion is
   **refused**, never skipped. A route whose picture never changes is refused too.
4. **The game** is built (`prepare_game` writes `sg1000_game.h`, the cartridge as the checked runs saw it as resource 103, the notices of the compiled
   components as 203, the identity as 205, the Windows metadata and the icon: the cover the player's options ask for, found as on the other machines;
   `sg1000_build.project_cmake(game=True)` builds the player, the machine, the fallback interpreter, the host kit, the bodies and SDL2 into one file).
5. **The delivered file** is the build compressed as publication does it (UPX when "Compress games" is on; the build as built when it is off or fails).
   It is replayed headless (`game.exe --headless`, from its own resources) on **every** route: every compared field must equal the reference's, every
   frame row must equal the reference's byte for byte, and its counts of native and interpreted steps must equal the native check's, or the
   conversion is refused and nothing is published. If only the compressed copy fails, the build as built is replayed and delivered unpacked, with
   the notice (`retrorecomp/delivery_evidence.py`).
6. **The quick states** of the delivered file are checked in fresh processes (`check_states`, layout `sg1000-state-2`): a state saved near the end of
   the `play` route is loaded by a new process, which continues the same 600 frames (rows equal); thirteen damaged, cut or foreign copies and a missing
   file are refused (exit 4), left as they were, and nothing is created.
7. **The report** (schema 2, writer `sg1000_conversion`, marker `sg1000_checks`) counts the interpreter fallback per route in instructions and cycles,
   says `delivered: true`, names the delivered file (`packed` or `unpacked`, the digest of the bytes replayed, the executable's name), the quick-state
   checks, the notices and the artwork, and the delivered file is copied into the output folder under the game's name.

What this measures: native code (instructions run natively and on the counted interpreter) and the agreement of the native run, and of the delivered
file, with the project's own reference interpreter on the routes played. It does not measure CPU fidelity against an independent oracle, hardware
fidelity, gameplay or physical latency, and says so in the report (`hardware_fidelity`, `gameplay` and `latency` stay not validated or not measured).
Nobody has played a delivered game: the replays are headless and the window is checked only on an authored cartridge by the self-test below.

## 3. The five measures

Kept separate here, as everywhere. Interpreter fallback will always be counted, per CPU (the Z80), and reported by the conversion.

| Measure | What is known | What is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native code | Measured per conversion on the routes played: Z80 instructions run natively and on the counted interpreter. A developer conversion of one real cartridge ran its two routes (3,600 frames each) with no interpreted instruction, in the native check and in the delivered game's headless replays alike; that says nothing about another game or another route | The share on routes not played, and any other cartridge |
| CPU fidelity | The native run equals the project's own reference interpreter on the routes played (state, RAM, picture and sound of every frame). The independent SingleStepTests/z80 vectors (51,328, 22,704 encodings) are compiled through this machine's own build of the generator (`tools/banked_vector_selftest.py --machine sg1000`, no wait state): 51,328 pass, 0 fail, 0 miss (22 November 2026). They are an emulator's vectors on a flat test bus, so the region map's dispatch is covered by the games, not by them | Hardware captures of the chip; the bus waveforms |
| Hardware fidelity | Nothing is known yet; nothing is claimed | The video chip's behaviour on this board, the sound chip, the ports and the RAM mirror, against an independent reference |
| Gameplay validation | Nothing is known yet: a delivered game has been replayed headless and run in a hidden window for a bounded number of frames, never played by a person | Everything |
| Physical latency | Nothing is known yet; not measured | No latency figure; none is claimed |

## 4. Controls

The game (stage 3) has the console's own controls: the D-pad, buttons 1 and 2 on two pads, and the **Pause** button, which is the console's NMI.
They are the Master System's keys, so a player of both finds the same hands.

| Action | Player 1 (keyboard) | Player 2 (keyboard) | Controller |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up, Down, Left, Right | arrow keys | numeric keypad 5, 2, 1, 3 | D-pad (and the left stick) |
| Button 1 | **S** | keypad 8 | A |
| Button 2 | **D** | keypad 9 | B |
| Pause | **Enter** | keypad 7 | Start |

Pause is bit 6 of each seat's word, an ordinary action row: the machine raises its /NMI once on the press edge (from the word it kept at the last
frame, part of its state), as the console's button does, and the game's own pause routine runs (the kit pauses nothing by itself; this is not
the Pause menu of the Master System). It is the same path alone and in a match. Every key and button can be changed in F2
(see [CONTROLS.md](CONTROLS.md)); F1 lists them, F6 is autofire for the two buttons, F8 and F9 are the quick states.

**Quick states.** F8 saves and F9 loads one state per game, in the game's own folder (`datas/games/sg1000-<identity>/quick.rrstate`): the CPU, the 1 KiB
of RAM, the video chip (VRAM, registers, the picture and the border of each line), the sound chip and the machine's clock, field by field in a fixed
byte order (layout `sg1000-state-2`). A state is bound to the exact cartridge (its SHA-256), to the standard (NTSC or PAL) and to the build; one of
another game, standard or build, a damaged one and a missing one are refused with a notice, and the game goes on exactly as it was. It is
taken at the end of a frame, and a state loaded by a fresh process continues the same frames (checked, below).

**What the self-test covers, and what it does not.** `tools/sg1000_player_selftest.py` builds the game from an authored cartridge (no ROM) and checks:
(a) the game's headless replay of a route equals the native check's report, rows, sound and dumps, and the check equals the reference interpreter, in
NTSC and in PAL; (b) a state saved near the end of a route and loaded by a fresh process continues the same 600 frames, and every damaged, cut,
extended, foreign or crafted state is refused with nothing changed; (c) the game run windowed on a hidden window (SDL offscreen video, dummy audio)
presents a picture each frame that is not blank, answers a held direction and button and the Pause key, saves and loads in play, and a refused F9 leaves
the frames as a run that never asked. It does **not** check the sound of the window (the dummy device plays nothing), a real cartridge, a real
controller or display, or any timing against a console.

**Not done.** Multiplayer (netplay and the link): the adapter declares no snapshot, digest or session hook, and the profile says `netplay_session = False`.
No cartridge RAM (The Castle), no SC-3000 keyboard, no peripherals. The picture is the video chip's colour indices through a published approximation of its sixteen
colours (not measured on a set). No latency figure: the pads are taken at the end of each frame, right before the game's own vertical-blank interrupt, and
how long a press takes to reach a real screen is not measured.

## 6. The library this scope was drawn from

A study of November 2026 counted the owner's own set, hashes and sizes only, nothing copied: 68 cartridges, one `.sg` each in a ZIP, from 8 KiB to
48 KiB (5 of 8 KiB, 14 of 16 KiB, 2 of 24 KiB, 42 of 32 KiB, 4 of 40 KiB and 1 of 48 KiB). None is larger than the 48 KiB of cartridge space, so no
bank switching is involved. A private comparison emulator's own software list knows all 68 of them, and only one is not a plain ROM: The Castle, a
cartridge with its own RAM. The same list holds boards this scope leaves out (Dahjee RAM carts, Othello Multivision carts, BASIC and music
cartridges, a graphics-tablet cartridge), none among the 68. This says what the owner's library holds; it is not a game list and not a compatibility claim.

## 7. Where it stands in the qualification

The `sg1000` column of [QUALIFICATION.md](QUALIFICATION.md) was read on a panel of eight games on 22 November 2026: `Y` on G2 to G6, G8, G9, G10, G12 and G13, `P` on G1, G11 and G15, `-` on G7 (no cartridge save), `-` on G16 (path sharing declined: nothing to share, 0 interpreted steps and no learned pattern on the 16 games of the two panels), `?` on G14 (the owner's). A conversion now delivers a game and
produces the evidence of two gates for the one cartridge it converted (the delivered file replayed against the reference, the quick states in a fresh
process), but the gates are read on a panel, not on one conversion. The machine is
`experimental` and `developer_only`, with `linux = 'pending'`. Leaving the experimental status is the owner's own decision (G14) on a very high bar, long
after the stages below. The random routes are listed as declined in `tests/test_machine_registry.py` for now only because the routes of the conversion are not yet qualified
on a panel.

**MAME 0.289 as a private oracle** (21 and 22 November 2026, run apart from this repository on the owner's Flicky; nothing of MAME is in it):
- *the pad ports*: the twelve directions and buttons of both pads, held alone, read at DC and DD exactly as in MAME (`tools/sg1000_player_selftest.py`,
  proof (e), `MAME_PADS`), idle reads FF on both;
- *Pause*: one /NMI per press whatever the press lasts (a one-frame and a ten-frame press give one each), as the machine's edge in the pad word does;
- *the frame*: on Flicky's attract with no input, VRAM and RAM hashes are equal to MAME's on each of the first 65 frames, then RAM differs. The whole
  difference is the **frame phase**: MAME's frame starts 28 lines before the VDP's line 0 and sets the frame flag some tens of cycles later in the
  line. With that phase (a start at line 234 and a delay of 80 to 227 cycles) the 80 frames' RAM writes are identical, in order. Our phase is the
  other one and **is not changed**: the choice (and MAME's IX and IY of FFFF at power on, ours 0, both undefined on the chip) is the maintainer's, and
  nothing says which one a real console has. These agree with an emulator, not with a console.

## 8. Multiplayer

A match is two seats in rollback (docs/MULTIPLAYER.md): the host kit saves one in-process snapshot a frame (the state's body without the picture,
`sg1000_snap_*` in `sg1000_state_file.inc`) and compares a digest of it. The pad word carries the stick, the two buttons and Pause. There is no
cartridge save to share. `--snap-probe AT:N` of the delivered game (headless) saves a snapshot, runs N frames, loads it and runs the same frames
again: every digest and picture is compared (`tools/sg1000_player_selftest.py`, proof (d)).
