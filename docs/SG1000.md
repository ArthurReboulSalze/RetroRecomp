# SG-1000

Profile `sg1000`: the Sega SG-1000 and SG-1000 II, **experimental, developer only, in development**. This is stage 1 of the machine
([NEW_MACHINE.md](NEW_MACHINE.md)): the converter recognises an SG-1000 cartridge file, reads it and **converts nothing yet**. A conversion
is refused with a sentence before anything is written. Nothing on this page says that a game will play, that the hardware will be reproduced
faithfully or how long input will take to reach the screen: those are five separate measures, and for this machine none is known yet.

## 1. What is converted, and what is not

Stage 1 converts nothing. The scope below is the one the machine is built for.

| In scope | Out of scope (with the reason) |
| --- | --- |
| SG-1000 and SG-1000 II cartridges (the two consoles run the same software), plain ROM of 2 KiB to 48 KiB, read from a `.sg`, from a `.bin` or `.rom` that you choose as SG-1000 or keep in a folder named `sg1000`, `sg-1000` or `sg_1000`, or from a ZIP holding one | **SC-3000**: its keyboard, its BASIC cartridges and its cassette are another machine's work |
| Two pads (the D-pad and buttons 1 and 2) and the Pause button, which is the console's NMI | **Othello Multivision, Terebi Oekaki** (a graphics tablet), **Dahjee** RAM-expansion cartridges, Card Catcher, Music Editor and the other peripherals and boards: a cartridge that needs one is refused with a clear message |
| NTSC and PAL timing, chosen per game as on the Master System | **SK-1100 keyboard, SP-400 plotter, FM Sound Unit** and paddle-style controllers, unless a first census shows a game that needs one |
| The cartridge RAM of one board of the library studied (The Castle), in a later stage | A file larger than 48 KiB, or not a whole number of kilobytes: a bank-switched or unknown board, refused by the reader |

The reader (`retrorecomp/sg1000_rom.py`) accepts a whole number of kilobytes from 2 KiB to 48 KiB, alone or as the only cartridge of a ZIP. An SG-1000
dump has no header, so a 512-byte copier header is **not** removed: such a file is refused as a size that is not a whole number of kilobytes. The
file is read in place and never changed; Retro-Recomp publishes no game. A cartridge's bytes do not say that it is an SG-1000 cartridge, so the
extension `.sg`, the folder or your choice in the menu decides.

## 2. How it is planned to work

1. **Native code.** The cartridge is translated ahead of time to C by the shared banked Z80 chain (`retrorecomp/z80_chain.py`, the one the Master
   System, the Game Gear and the MSX family use) on the machine's own description, `SG1000_MACHINE` in `retrorecomp/systems/sg1000.py`: eight 8 KiB
   regions of the 48 KiB cartridge space, no wait state on the M1 cycle, /INT read at the end of each instruction and the /NMI pin of the Pause button
   (the chain learned that pin for this machine and the ColecoVision). What the translation does not cover will run on the reference Z80
   interpreter and be counted, per CPU.
2. **The machine.** The TMS9918A video chip is the shared module `native/tms9918/` that the MSX family compiles too; the sound chip, the 1 KiB of RAM,
   the ports and the pads are the machine's own C, to be written. No emulator core is meant to be inside the executable.
3. **No firmware.** An SG-1000 cartridge boots by itself: no BIOS is read, needed or shipped.

What exists today is the profile, the cartridge reader, the machine's description, the registries, this page, the machine's C
(`native/sg1000_machine/`, with the TMS9918A and the SN76489A of `native/tms9918` and `native/sn76489`) and, from stage 3, the game itself: its
player (`sg1000_player.c`), its host-kit adapter (`native/hostkit/adapters/rrhk_sg1000.inc`) and its quick states
(`sg1000_state_file.inc`), checked ROM-free by `tools/sg1000_player_selftest.py` on an authored cartridge. The conversion
(`retrorecomp/sg1000_conversion.py`) is still a refusal here: no real cartridge has been made into a game, and nothing on this page says a real game
plays.

## 3. The five measures

Kept separate here, as everywhere. Interpreter fallback will always be counted, per CPU (the Z80), and reported by the conversion.

| Measure | What is known | What is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native code | Nothing is known yet: no game is converted | Everything |
| CPU fidelity | Nothing is known yet. The Z80 chain it will use passes the independent SingleStepTests/z80 vectors on the other machines that use it, which says nothing about this machine's own region map | Everything about this machine |
| Hardware fidelity | Nothing is known yet; nothing is claimed | The video chip's behaviour on this board, the sound chip, the ports and the RAM mirror, against an independent reference |
| Gameplay validation | Nothing is known yet: no game has been run, let alone played | Everything |
| Physical latency | Nothing is known yet; not measured | No latency figure; none is claimed |

## 4. Controls

The game (stage 3) has the console's own controls: the D-pad, buttons 1 and 2 on two pads, and the **Pause** button, which is the console's NMI.
They are the Master System's keys, so a player of both finds the same hands.

| Action | Player 1 (keyboard) | Player 2 (keyboard) | Controller |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up, Down, Left, Right | arrow keys | numeric keypad 5, 2, 1, 3 | D-pad (and the left stick) |
| Button 1 | **S** | keypad 8 | A |
| Button 2 | **D** | keypad 9 | B |
| Pause | **Enter** | keypad 7 | Start |

Pause is the host's own action, not a bit of the pad: a press raises the machine's /NMI once, as the console's button does, and the game's own
pause routine runs (the kit pauses nothing by itself; this is not the Pause menu of the Master System). Every key and button can be changed in F2
(see [CONTROLS.md](CONTROLS.md)); F1 lists them, F6 is autofire for the two buttons, F8 and F9 are the quick states.

**Quick states.** F8 saves and F9 loads one state per game, in the game's own folder (`datas/games/sg1000-<identity>/quick.rrstate`): the CPU, the 1 KiB
of RAM, the video chip (VRAM, registers, the picture and the border of each line), the sound chip and the machine's clock, field by field in a fixed
byte order (layout `sg1000-state-1`). A state is bound to the exact cartridge (its SHA-256), to the standard (NTSC or PAL) and to the build; one of
another game, standard or build, a damaged one and a missing one are refused with a notice, and the game goes on exactly as it was. It is
taken at the end of a frame, and a state loaded by a fresh process continues the same frames (checked, below).

**What the self-test covers, and what it does not.** `tools/sg1000_player_selftest.py` builds the game from an authored cartridge (no ROM) and checks:
(a) the game's headless replay of a route equals the native check's report, rows, sound and dumps, and the check equals the reference interpreter, in
NTSC and in PAL; (b) a state saved near the end of a route and loaded by a fresh process continues the same 600 frames, and every damaged, cut,
extended, foreign or crafted state is refused with nothing changed; (c) the game run windowed on a hidden window (SDL offscreen video, dummy audio)
presents a picture each frame that is not blank, answers a held direction and button and the Pause key, saves and loads in play, and a refused F9 leaves
the frames as a run that never asked. It does **not** check the sound of the window (the dummy device plays nothing), a real cartridge, a real
controller or display, or any timing against a console.

**Not done.** Multiplayer (netplay and the link): the adapter declares no snapshot, digest or session hook, and the profile says `netplay_session = False`.
No cartridge RAM (The Castle), no SC-3000 keyboard, no peripherals. The picture is the video chip's colour indices through a published approximation of its sixteen
colours (not measured on a set). No latency figure: the pads are taken at the end of each frame, right before the game's own vertical-blank interrupt, and
how long a press takes to reach a real screen is not measured.

## 5. Licences

Everything written for this machine is original Retro-Recomp code, MIT: the profile, the reader and the host-kit adapter. The route has no GPL code and
needs no other licence decision: the video chip is our own C shared with the MSX, the sound chip will be our own C, and the player's cartridges are read
in place. The Z80 translator and the reference Z80 interpreter are the ones of the Master System, the Game Gear and the MSX, under their own licences,
listed in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) (smsggrecomp, z80-recomp-core, superzazu/z80). MAME is run privately later, as a separate program, for
comparisons only; nothing of it is in an executable or in this repository. The upstreams are in [UPSTREAMS.md](UPSTREAMS.md).

## 6. The library this scope was drawn from

A study of November 2026 counted the owner's own set, hashes and sizes only, nothing copied: 68 cartridges, one `.sg` each in a ZIP, from 8 KiB to
48 KiB (5 of 8 KiB, 14 of 16 KiB, 2 of 24 KiB, 42 of 32 KiB, 4 of 40 KiB and 1 of 48 KiB). None is larger than the 48 KiB of cartridge space, so no
bank switching is involved. A private comparison emulator's own software list knows all 68 of them, and only one is not a plain ROM: The Castle, a
cartridge with its own RAM. The same list holds boards this scope leaves out (Dahjee RAM carts, Othello Multivision carts, BASIC and music
cartridges, a graphics-tablet cartridge), none among the 68. This says what the owner's library holds; it is not a game list and not a compatibility claim.

## 7. Where it stands in the qualification

The `sg1000` column of [QUALIFICATION.md](QUALIFICATION.md) is all `?`: no gate has been looked at, because there is nothing to convert. The machine is
`experimental` and `developer_only`, with `linux = 'pending'`. Leaving the experimental status is the owner's own decision (G14) on a very high bar, long
after the stages below. The random routes are listed as declined in `tests/test_machine_registry.py` for now only because the conversion that will write them does not
exist yet.

## 8. What comes next

The conversion on the banked Z80 chain and a first game, then the qualification gates on a small panel, then the host-kit adapter and multiplayer, an
optimisation pass and the release kit, in the order of [NEW_MACHINE.md](NEW_MACHINE.md). No date is promised. A record kind for the path sharing and its
line in `native/runtime_feedback.h` are decided with the conversion.
